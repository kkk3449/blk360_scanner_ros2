# Response to Reviewer 2 (electronics-4525706, Revision 1)


We thank the reviewer for identifying the places where the paper's claims required tighter scoping and stronger evidence. In this revision we (i) moved every corrective module into the Methods section with frozen definitions, (ii) added an independent matching scorecard for the real three-epoch study, (iii) re-scoped the zero-cost re-verification claim to what the data actually shows, and (iv) added a sample-flow table connecting every evaluation set. Locations refer to the revised manuscript.

**Comments 1:** The geometric decomposition is explicitly non-novel, while multi-view VLM classification and TOSM build on [30] and [13–15]. The paper should distinguish new algorithms from system integration and quantify the additions over these prior works.

**Response 1:** Agreed. Section 1 now contains an explicit novelty-scoping paragraph immediately after the contribution list: the base algorithms (RANSAC, DBSCAN, PCA modeling, open-vocabulary 3D classification, per-view VLM querying, the TOSM schema) are claimed as integration; the new mechanisms are the control structures around them (confidence-tier escalation with structural query gating, mint-once identity with history-preserving upserts, trigger-defined corrective layers, owner feedback as first-class time-variant evidence). Each addition is quantified against its base where one exists: corrective layers lift automated recall 28% → 85% over the raw backbone (Section 5.7); structural gating moves trap hallucination from 0/4 blocked (same graph, ungated) to 3–4/4 (Section 5.5); escalation adds +11.1/+6.3 accuracy points over single-pass fusion (Section 5.3); For context, the TOSM lineage reports 78–81% recognition accuracy under a different evaluation protocol, whereas our frozen re-run achieves 85% automated recall while additionally maintaining per-fact confidence and revision provenance (Section 5.7); we present these numbers as contextual rather than like-for-like comparisons.

**Comments 2:** The exact confidence-weighted fusion rule is not provided, and the fixed 0.6 threshold is not analyzed. The drop of unanimous precision to 57.1% in Table 9 shows that the verification tiers are scene-dependent.

**Response 2:** All three points are now addressed. The exact rule (per-label sum of view confidences; share = normalized confidence mass of the argmax) is in Section 4.4. The threshold is analyzed by re-scoring the stored votes across 0.40–0.90 (no new calls): 0.6 lies on a plateau, F1 peaks on a broad 0.45–0.65 band on all three scenes, and no threshold dominates 0.6 beyond single-object noise (Section 5.3, "Threshold sensitivity"). On scene dependence we agree and now say so directly: the same sweep shows the cafeteria shifts the whole curve downward without changing its shape — the threshold cannot repair segmentation- or render-level failures — and the new per-tier recall/F1 table (Table in Section 5.3) quantifies the tiers' scene dependence explicitly (combined-verified recall 0.722/0.781 on the industrial scenes versus 0.250 on the cafeteria). The tiers are presented as empirically validated on the development scenes and stress-bounded on the cafeteria, not as scene-invariant guarantees.

**Comments 3:** Table 4 does not adequately validate the segmentation backbone. Its proxy metrics do not measure instance quality, and semantic PTv3 outputs are not directly comparable with DBSCAN instances. The parameter sweep is also evaluated against the operating-point decomposition itself.

**Response 3:** We agree on all three sub-points and have revised accordingly. The caption and text of Table 4 now state explicitly that its metrics are proxies that do not measure instance quality, and the PTv3 row is marked semantic-only and not instance-comparable — the table's role is downgraded to backbone screening, not validation. On circularity, the sweep was re-scored against an independent, membership-independent reference (all clean-cloud points inside each owner-audited oriented box): on the robot hall the operating point remains at the grid maximum (recall 0.952 = 40/42) on an unchanged plateau, while on the showroom the audit uncovered that the original rows had been joined onto the operating run's own clusters — against the truly audited inventory no base-clustering setting exceeds recall 0.33, so those rows are now presented as evidence for the necessity of the split-refinement stage rather than as parameter sensitivity (Section 5.2 reports the correction plainly). The definitive backbone evidence is positioned as the end-to-end layered recall of Section 5.7 (28→85% against the owner-audited inventory), which does not reference the operating point.

**Comments 4:** The matching rules use fixed spatial, extent, type, and distance thresholds, but conflict resolution is unspecified. The real three-epoch study does not report complete matching accuracy, identity-switch, false-new, and false-absent statistics.

**Response 4:** Both gaps are closed. Conflict resolution is specified in Section 4.5 (greedy in deterministic record order; nearest horizontal centroid among qualifying nodes; first claim locks a node) together with the physical rationale for each threshold. For the statistics, we built an independent correspondence reference by bidirectional point overlap (5 cm association, both directions >= 0.5) between consecutive epochs and replayed the recorded upserts against it (Section 5.6, "Matching scorecard"): matching accuracy on strong reference pairs 2/4 (T1→T2) and 4/7 (T2→T3); identity switches 0 on both transitions; strict false-new/false-absent 2/2 and 3/3; nine split/merge cases reported separately; multi-candidate conflicts 0 (spatial pass) and 1 (move pass). Every miss is a fragmentation failure of the per-axis extent tolerance — the same object re-segmented to a different extent and re-inserted under a new ID — never a wrong association, and the scorecard is robust to doubling the association radius to 10 cm. We believe this is a more honest picture than a single accuracy number: the matcher's weakness is re-segmentation sensitivity, not identity confusion.

**Comments 5:** Zero-cost re-verification is supported by identical-input or controlled-repeat tests, while the 3.1% result comes from a controlled combined-edit scenario rather than the real T2-to-T3 transition.

**Response 5:** Correct, and the claim is now scoped accordingly in all four places it appeared (Abstract-adjacent intro, Sections 4.6, 5.6, and 7): the 3.1% figure is from the controlled same-quality re-scan; on the real T2→T3 transition the acquisition protocol itself changed, no cluster hash matched, and the epoch paid full verification cost ($3.54 for 41 clusters). The revised text states that zero-cost carry-over is a property of reproducible acquisition, not of the update mechanism alone.

**Comments 6:** Table 5 is statistically underpowered, gated querying still has a 40.6% hallucination rate on the robot hall, and Table 7 reaches 85% recall with only 51% precision and approximately 31% type accuracy.

**Response 6:** We accept all three characterizations and have made the paper state them plainly. Table 5 carries Wilson 95% intervals and the text reports the McNemar results as consistent improvement short of significance. The query benchmark now states its statistical scope explicitly (Section 5.5): at n=48/32 the gated-vs-ungated accuracy gap is a single question per scene, so the benchmark is diagnostic, and the case for gating rests on the trap mechanism, the abstention structure, the hallucination direction, and the mission-level consequences of Section 6 — not on accuracy deltas. The robot hall's 40.6% hallucination reflects upstream label errors propagating into every grounded condition, which we report as the honest end-to-end number; and Table 7's precision column is explicitly framed as the cost of recall (review burden), with type accuracy (~31%) attributed to the correlated verifier bias whose closure is the owner layer's role.

**Comments 7:** The evaluation uses several different cluster sets and denominators. A sample-flow table should connect the 38-object, 50-scored-cluster, 41-cluster, 35-cluster, and 46-object evaluation sets.

**Response 7:** Done — new Table (Section 5.1, "Evaluation-set lineage") connects every set to its derivation stage: the 57-cluster showroom decomposition → 50 GT-scored (36 confirmed); the separate 38-object reproducible det set; the T3 chain 79 → 41 room-scoped (owner audit set) → 26 filter survivors, the det2 precut chain 66 → 35 → 27-to-VLM, det3/det4 33 → 46, the cross-epoch graph's present-count trajectory across revisions 7–14 (ending at the 46-object graph), and the det5 frozen re-run's 35-in-room-vs-46 scoring. Every count was recomputed from the archived stage outputs (one log-only count is marked), and the Ground-truth paragraph cross-references the table so no denominator has to be inferred from prose.

**Comments 8:** Several corrective modules were developed from failures observed in the same environment used for retrospective evaluation. Their complete definitions should be moved to the methodology section, and the final frozen pipeline should be evaluated independently.

**Response 8:** The definitions have moved: new Section 4.7 defines all five corrective layers with triggers and parameters, including the panel square-up finishing pass of the wall-compound layer that appears as the last row of the cumulative-recall table (details in our Response 8 to Reviewer 1); Point-SAM refinement is now explicitly marked as an optional diagnostic outside the frozen pipeline; and Section 5 retains only results with cross-references. On independent evaluation, we sharpened what the paper can and cannot claim: Section 5.7 now states that the retrospective replay runs the frozen pipeline exactly as defined — every trigger and threshold fixed, no per-scene adjustment, no owner input at execution time — making it an independent run on a non-independent scene; the cafeteria is the only scene independent in both senses (and the one mechanically transferable layer, the hybrid structure filter, is checked held-out there: 2/2 ceiling-noise clusters flagged, zero false positives); and a multi-building evaluation is stated as future work in Section 7. We chose explicit scoping over adding a new building within the revision window, because a building annotated to a lower ground-truth standard than the rest of the paper would weaken rather than strengthen the evidence. We also note that Section 5.1 now states threshold provenance in two parts: base-pipeline constants fixed on T1/T2, corrective-layer thresholds developed on the test-room lineage (including the T3 audit) and frozen before the cafeteria.

# Response to Reviewer 3 (electronics-4525706, Revision 1)


We thank the reviewer for a structured assessment that both located the paper's contribution accurately — a confidence-controlled, rectifiable, and identity-preserving semantic maintenance architecture rather than a new segmentation algorithm, a characterization we adopt gratefully — and enumerated concrete gaps. We respond to each of the seven report items below, quoting the report verbatim and addressing every sub-point; all section numbers refer to the revised manuscript.

**Comments 1:** What is the fundamental question addressed by the research? The research question of the article is: How can geometric data obtained from registered terrestrial laser scans be transformed into a queryable semantic knowledge graph—one that prevents unverified semantic labels from influencing robot decisions and allows for updates while preserving object identities as the environment changes?

**Response 1:** We confirm that this is precisely the question the paper addresses, and we have adopted the reviewer's formulation in spirit when tightening the claims: the revised Abstract and Conclusion now state the identity-preservation result as "zero observed identity switches on strong reference correspondences, while re-segmentation still produces false-new/false-absent cases," so that the answer to the question is stated at the strength the evidence supports (Section 5.6, matching scorecard).

**Comments 2:** Is the topic original and timely; does it address a genuine research gap? The topic of the article is timely. It holds critical importance in the fields of robotics, 3D semantic mapping, VLM verification, knowledge graphs, and long-term environment modeling. The article aims to present a novel system by integrating components known individually—namely TLS processing, DBSCAN, open-set classification, VLM voting, knowledge graphs, and change matching. Essentially, it does not propose a new fundamental classification or segmentation algorithm; rather, it introduces a confidence-controlled, rectifiable, and identity-preserving semantic maintenance architecture. Future studies should be conducted on a wider variety of buildings within industrial environments.

**Response 2:** We agree with this characterization and have made it explicit in the paper. Section 1 now contains a novelty-scoping paragraph immediately after the contribution list: the base algorithms (RANSAC, DBSCAN, PCA modeling, open-vocabulary 3D classification, per-view VLM querying, the TOSM schema) are claimed as integration, and the new mechanisms are the control structures around them — confidence-tier escalation with structural query gating, mint-once identity with history-preserving upserts, trigger-defined corrective layers, and owner feedback as first-class, time-variant evidence — each quantified against its base where one exists. The multi-building recommendation is addressed under Response 4 and is stated as future work in Section 7.

**Comments 3:** Contribution of the study compared to previous work. The primary contribution is the consistent integration of known components around trust status, data provenance, revision history, and persistent object identity. The claims made in the paper have generally been substantiated. However, the claim regarding a new dataset/benchmark was insufficient. Furthermore, the raw scans were not shared. It was not demonstrated that the deterministic geometric backbone remains deterministic across different operating systems, library versions, or hardware configurations. The trust-based query gate was constructed using a small dataset, which was deemed insufficient.

**Response 3:** *Dataset/benchmark claim and raw scans.* Both addressed. On the claim: we agree that the original wording could have suggested a stronger benchmark contribution than the evidence supported. The revised manuscript therefore makes clear that the released materials constitute a documented evaluation protocol and dataset, not a community benchmark; the word "benchmark" is now used only for our internal four-condition query evaluation. What was genuinely insufficient was data access, and that is now fixed: the raw E57 scans for T1–T3 and the cafeteria, together with the wall-removed T1 processing input (~5.7 GB total), are deposited on Zenodo under CC-BY 4.0 with SHA-256 checksums (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22590752), and the Data Availability statement now cites the deposit alongside the existing GitHub release of ground truth, query benchmarks, and pipeline code.

*Cross-platform determinism.* We built a version-pinned container kit for exactly this check and report two cross-platform results in Section 5.2. First, across software stacks: running the backbone under a deliberately different environment (glibc 2.41 vs. 2.39, Python 3.12.14 vs. 3.12.3) reproduces all 81 retained robot-hall clusters byte-identically to the native run, and consecutive containerized runs are byte-identical to each other. Second, across hardware: the same container executed on a second machine with a different CPU vendor and microarchitecture (Intel Core i9-13900H laptop vs. the AMD Ryzen 9 9900X reference workstation) reproduces the identical per-cluster checksums and aggregate hash. The kit (container recipe, reference checksums, comparison script) ships with the data package so the check is repeatable by others. We also scope the claim precisely in the revised text: bit-level determinism is demonstrated across operating environments and x86-64 CPU vendors under the pinned library set; reproducibility across instruction-set architectures is not claimed.

*Small query-gate dataset.* Acknowledged and now stated in the paper's own voice (Section 5.5): at n=48/32 questions the benchmark is diagnostic rather than statistically powered — the gated-vs-ungated accuracy gap is one question per scene — and the case for gating rests on the trap-blocking mechanism, the abstention structure, the hallucination direction, and the mission-level consequences of Section 6. Question counts, category composition, and abstention accounting are now printed in the table itself (see also Response 7).

**Comments 4:** Necessary methodological improvements. The methodology section of the article is presented in detail. However, the evaluations were based on errors observed in a specific environment, and testing was conducted within that same building. There is insufficient evidence to demonstrate whether the study would achieve the same level of success in different buildings. A test set comprising multiple buildings and independent facilities should be included. The article should also present the object class distribution for the scenes and the results for each class. Object records, the matching function, confidence states, and upsert decisions have not been consolidated under a single formal definition. It is not clearly explained which dataset—training, development, or test—was used to determine the thresholds. Ground truth labels should be generated in a blind manner by independent evaluators who do not see the model's suggestions. Controlled experiments regarding registration noise, voxel resolution, and color variation should be included. The metrics for Top-1 accuracy, per-tier precision, hallucination rate, and updates are considered quite reasonable. However, Recall/F1 per verification tier is missing. Metrics such as task success rate, path length, latency, and safe stopping are also missing. End-to-end task validation on a real robot is not provided.

**Response 4:** *Single building; multi-building test set.* We agree this is the central external-validity limitation and have scoped the paper accordingly rather than adding a new building within the revision window, because a building annotated to a lower ground-truth standard than the rest of the paper (owner-audited inventories) would weaken rather than strengthen the evidence. What the revision adds: Section 5.7 now states explicitly that the retrospective replay is an independent *run* (frozen pipeline, no owner input) on a non-independent *scene*; the cafeteria — a different floor, different function, different furnishing and floor material — is identified as the only scene independent in both senses, with the mechanically transferable layer checked held-out on it; and Section 7 states the multi-building evaluation as future work.

*Class distribution and per-class results.* Added — a new class-distribution table (Section 5.3) gives the ground truth for all four evaluation sets grouped into eight families with per-family verification outcomes. It makes the paper's central failure mode quantitative: robots are the worst family in every robot-bearing set (0/4, 0/3, 0/8 correct — vocabulary-gap absorption), displays and fixtures abstain heavily, and the T3 owner audit's 6/25/10 (correct/wrong/abstained) distribution is shown per family.

*Single formal definition of records, matching, confidence states, upserts.* Done — Section 4.6 now ends with a "Formal summary": the record tuple r = (a, p, d, l, c, tau, pi), the seven-element tier set with its evidence ordering, the match predicate m(n, r) with both variants, and the deterministic match → move → insert → absent cascade.

*Which dataset determined the thresholds.* Now stated in Section 5.1 (Controls) in two parts, because the honest answer differs by component: constants of the base pipeline (Sections 4.1–4.6) were fixed on the development epochs T1/T2 and applied unchanged to T3 and the cafeteria; the corrective-layer thresholds of Section 4.7 were developed on the test-room lineage — including failures revealed by the T3 owner audit — and were then frozen before evaluation on the cafeteria stress scene, so T3 is treated as a retrospective development-scene replay of those layers rather than a held-out test. Nothing is trained, so the meaningful split is development room vs. held-out cafeteria, and Section 5.7 states which claims survive on which side.

*Blind ground truth by independent evaluators.* Addressed with a blinded secondary annotation — we note candidly that the annotator is a co-author who had seen neither the pipeline output nor the owner audit, not an external evaluator, and the revised text says so. This annotator labeled all 41 T3 clusters blind, from full-resolution multi-view render sheets containing only neutral shuffled IDs (free vocabulary, per-item confidence). Blind labels agree with the owner ground truth on 31 of the 33 clusters both annotators labeled (93.9%; both disagreements are low-confidence blind labels), and — the anchoring-specific test — the blind annotator independently reproduced the owner's overrides of the model label on 25 of 26 scored cases, versus 6 of 7 where the owner had accepted the model. Section 5.1 reports this and concludes that the owner corrections are not primarily anchoring-driven, while noting that the accepted subset is small and residual anchoring cannot be excluded; the original disclosure of the anchoring channel is retained. The kit, answers, and scoring are in the data package.

*Registration noise, voxel resolution, color variation.* Added (Section 5.8, "Voxel resolution and registration noise"): a voxel-resolution sweep (2/3/5 cm) and a seeded per-point Gaussian registration-noise sweep (sigma = 5/10/20 mm) on the robot-hall backbone, scored per object against the owner-grounded reference. The results are informative in both directions: voxel size proves to be part of a jointly calibrated triple with the clustering parameters (recall 42/42 at the 3 cm baseline collapses to 0.36 at 2 cm via over-merging and 0.33 at 5 cm via small-object starvation), and noise at the scanner's real bundle-error scale (5 mm) already stresses the backbone (recall 0.48) with breakdown at 4x that level (0.07), the dominant mechanism being saturation of the 5 cm RANSAC inlier band that leaves wall residue bridging objects into merges. The revised text also delimits the regime honestly: per-point scatter is an artificial stress, since the real ~5 cm inter-epoch ICP residual is a rigid-transform error absorbed at the knowledge-graph matching level, not per-point noise. On color variation we must be precise about what the paper does and does not isolate. The existing render-source ablation (voxel-colored vs. full-resolution re-associated renders, both verifiers, three scenes — Section 5.8) is an appearance-quality control, but it changes point density and silhouette detail together with color, so it is not a pure photometric control. The revised text states this explicitly and lists a color-only perturbation study (deterministic brightness/saturation/temperature changes on identical renders) as open work; we did not add it within the revision window rather than present a confounded experiment as a color control.

*Recall/F1 per tier; task success, path length, latency, safe stopping.* Both added. Per-tier precision/recall/F1 for all three scenes are in the new table in Section 5.3 — including the honest number that combined-verified recall is 0.722/0.781 on the industrial scenes but 0.250 on the cafeteria, where the gate abstained on half the confirmed objects. Mission-level metrics are now stated as metrics in Section 6: task success 9/9, mean leg path lengths 4.6/5.4/1.8 m, completion times 16.5/24.9/15.0 s, semantic-resolution latency under one second with zero-motion refusal of gated goals, and the low-battery preemption behavior as the safety-stop mechanism.

*End-to-end validation on a real robot.* Correct, and the paper now says so unambiguously in three places: the Figure 19 caption ("these runs demonstrate the knowledge-graph-to-mission interface in a physics simulation; they do not substitute for validation on a physical robot"), Section 6 (scope statement), and Section 7 (limitation and future work).

**Comments 5:** Are the results consistent with the presented evidence? The results are generally consistent with the presented evidence. The experiments should be expanded. Reliability in general industrial environments is not supported. If manipulation is possible, it should be tested experimentally.

**Response 5:** We thank the reviewer for the assessment of consistency, which this revision has worked to strengthen further: every headline claim (identity preservation, zero-cost re-verification, threshold provenance, color variation) is now stated at exactly the strength its evidence supports, in the Abstract, Introduction, Methods, Discussion, and Conclusion alike. The experiments were expanded as described under Responses 3 and 4 (cross-platform determinism, class-level results, blinded secondary annotation, voxel/noise robustness, per-tier recall/F1, mission metrics), plus a threshold sweep, an independent matching scorecard, and a no-provisional-label ablation requested by the other reviewers. Reliability in general industrial environments is explicitly not claimed: Section 7 (Scope) states that results come from one scanner and one building, with the cafeteria as a bound rather than a validation. Manipulation is outside the validated scope — the pick behavior exists only as a stub interface in the behavior tree — and Section 7 now states this explicitly rather than testing a partial capability.

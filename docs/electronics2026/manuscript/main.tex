\begin{abstract}
Indoor service and inspection robots need environment knowledge
that is queryable, reliable, and maintainable as the site changes, yet
open-vocabulary 3D pipelines propagate unverified labels downstream and
build-once scene models offer no maintenance path. We present TLS-SMF, a
TLS-driven semantic modeling framework that converts registered terrestrial
laser scans into a confidence-aware semantic knowledge graph and keeps it
current across repeated scans. TLS-SMF combines a deterministic geometric
backbone whose re-runs are byte-identical on the same host; multi-view
vision--language-model verification with automatic escalation, which
retains unresolved objects as explicitly unverified; and an
identity-preserving incremental update that applies confidence-gated
node-level upserts to mint-once node identities without rebuilding the
graph. On two industrial indoor scenes, escalation lifts verification
accuracy from 75.0\% to 86.1\% and from 78.1\% to 84.4\%, with
94.4--100\% precision for unanimous votes; on the showroom scene,
structurally gated querying answers 83.3\% of benchmark questions versus
52.1\% for an ungrounded LLM while blocking unverified-label hallucination
traps; and selective re-verification prices a changed re-scan at 3.1\% of a
full rebuild (0\% when unchanged). A real three-epoch study with a
controlled-change re-scan detects 2/2 physical changes at geometry level
while preserving object identity across all three epochs.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} terrestrial laser scanning; semantic mapping; knowledge
graph; vision--language models; incremental update; mobile robots
